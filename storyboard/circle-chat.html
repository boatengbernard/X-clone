Your circle, as a group chat

Today a circle is one long page: the week, today's reading, a post box and a list of messages underneath. This turns the talking part into a group chat that works the way WhatsApp groups do (chat list, bubbles, replies, reactions, typing, "seen by"), while the reading stays pinned at the top so the chat never buries it. It uses the messaging component you sent as the base, rebuilt inside Selah and changed from one-to-one chats to groups.

Circles becomes a chat list

The Circles tab opens on your chats, like WhatsApp's Chats screen. Your current circle sits at the top with its unread count.

  • Each row shows the last message with the sender's name, the time, and a gold unread badge.
  • Search and the All / Unread / Prayer filters come from your component. "Prayer" replaces its "Pinned" filter and shows only prayer requests.
  • Finished circles move to a quieter "Finished" section so you can still scroll back through them.
  • The + button starts a private chat with someone in your circle, such as your guide.

From your component: thread list, unread badges, pin icon, search, filter chips, new-conversation button.

The group chat, with today's reading pinned

Opening the circle drops you into the conversation. Today's reading is pinned under the header, like a pinned message in WhatsApp, so it is always one tap away.

  • Your messages sit on the right in a warm gold bubble. Everyone else's are on the left with their name in their own colour, as in a WhatsApp group.
  • A date divider ("TODAY") and small centred notes for events: "Kofi and Ama finished Day 12 · 4 of 6 have read". These replace today's week card.
  • When you finish a reading, the one line you share arrives as a bubble labelled "Day 12".
  • Prayer requests are labelled bubbles with a "Praying · 4" count. Tapping it adds you, as the Pray button does today.
  • Two ticks on your own messages turn green when everyone has seen them.

From your component: chat header, bubbles, date dividers, delivery ticks (sent, delivered, read).

The + button: what you can send

Typing a message is the normal case. The + in the message box opens WhatsApp's attach sheet, filled with Selah's own kinds of message.

  • Today's reading shares the day's passage and your one line, the same as finishing a reading today.
  • Prayer request sends a labelled bubble others can mark "Praying".
  • Verse opens the Bible picker and drops in a verse card, in the translation you read.
  • Encourage someone lets you pick a member. They're mentioned by name, which replaces the guide's "Encourage" button.
  • Photos and voice notes are shown greyed out as "later". They need storage and moderation that Selah doesn't have yet.
  • "Esi is typing…" shows in the header and as three dots in the chat.

From your component: typing indicator, auto-growing message box, send button that swaps in when you type.

Press and hold: react, reply, pray

Pressing and holding a message lifts it and dims the rest of the chat, as in WhatsApp. A row of reactions sits above it and a menu below.

  • The five reactions are chosen for a Bible group: Praying, Amen, Love, With you, Glory. Each one shows a small label, so nobody has to guess what an emoji means.
  • Reply quotes the message inside your answer, which keeps a busy group readable (see screen 5).
  • Save to Journal keeps the message in your private Journal, next to your saved posts.
  • Seen by lists who has read it. Report works as it does today, and reported messages still go to the guide.
  • On a computer, the same menu opens from a small arrow that appears when you hover over a message.

Replies, verse cards and keeping people safe

A reply carries a quote of the message it answers, with the original sender's name in their colour. Verses arrive as cards rather than pasted text.

  • Tapping a quote scrolls back to the original message and highlights it for a moment.
  • The safety check you already have moves into the message box. If someone types words about self-harm, a help card appears above the box before they send. The guide is still alerted privately, as now.
  • Reactions gather in a small pill on the corner of the bubble. Tapping it shows who reacted.

Group info: who's reading, and your settings

Tapping the circle's name opens Group info, like WhatsApp's. Here, the member list shows each person's week of reading.

  • Each member has five dots for the week: green for read, faded gold for a grace day, empty for not yet. These replace today's week card and progress bar.
  • The guide sees "Quiet for 3 days" under a member, and tapping that member offers "Send encouragement", which opens a private chat.
  • Mute, your reminder time, everything shared (verses and prayer requests) and Leave circle all live here, instead of at the bottom of the page.
  • The note that circle messages are private and never reach the public feed moves here too, and appears once at the top of every new chat.

What stays the same

  • Joining: the join flow (pick a plan, your rhythm, reminder time) is unchanged. It ends by opening the new chat.
  • Reading: tapping Read on the pinned bar opens today's reading screen exactly as now. "Done" sends your line to the chat.
  • Privacy and safety: circle chats stay private, reports still go to the guide, crisis words still show help and alert the guide.
  • Data: all of it stays on the phone the way the rest of Selah does today. Real-time chat between different people's phones needs a server, which is a separate step.

Questions for you

  1. Private chats. Should people be able to message one member privately (screen 1's guide chat), or only the whole group?
  2. "Seen by" and ticks. WhatsApp shows who has read a message. Is that welcome in a prayer group, or does it add pressure? It could be on for the guide only.
  3. Reactions. Are Praying, Amen, Love, With you and Glory the right five?
  4. Where the week shows. Should the week of dots be only in Group info (screen 6), or also as a thin strip under the pinned reading?
